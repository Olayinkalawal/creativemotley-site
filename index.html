      Method

      Everything we make passes through one instrument, and one way of working.

      1. Front elementThe first glass the light meets.
      2. Lens elementsGroups of glass that bring the image into focus.
      3. ApertureOverlapping blades that set how much light gets through.
      4. Lens mountA bayonet with electrical contacts: lens and body talk through it.
      5. Body and gripDials, shutter button and the hand that holds them.
      6. Viewfinder (EVF)A small screen behind an eyepiece: the frame, at the eye.
      7. BatteryA removable pack. Carry a spare.
      8. ShutterTimes each exposure, just in front of the sensor.
      9. Image sensorWhere light becomes a picture.
      10. Stabiliser (IBIS)In-body image stabilisation: the sensor moves to cancel shake.
      11. Main boardThe processor that turns what the sensor sees into files.
      12. Rear screen (LCD)For framing, menus and playback.

      Process

      Seven steps, every time.

      1. Blank page

        The channel as it stands — nothing assumed.

        We audit what is already there — watch time, retention, the shape of the back catalogue.

      2. Moodboard

        Look, palette, references. What it should feel like.

        Palette, typography, thumbnail language. Agreed before a frame is shot.

      3. Storyboard

        Series design and upload calendar, mapped out.

        Series design and an upload calendar you can actually keep to.

      4. Production

        Shoot days, kit, crew — or your own, better run.

        Crew and kit where you need them, or your own team run properly.

      5. Sound

        Because bad audio loses the audience first.

        Mixed and mastered for phone speakers, because that is where it is heard.

      6. Edit

        Thumbnails, titles, metadata, cut to retain.

        Titles, tags, chapters and thumbnails — cut for retention, not for us.

      7. Published

        Then measured, and done again.

        Then measured against the audit, and the loop starts again.
